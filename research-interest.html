Research & publications

Research interest

My research lies at the interface of mathematics and biology, with a focus on how infectious diseases spread through populations and how they can be controlled. I build compartmental models of diseases such as monkeypox, dengue, Nipah virus, HFMD, COVID-19 and avian influenza. I formulate them with ordinary, fractional-order, delay and stochastic differential equations, partial differential equations, and analyse them with the tools of dynamical systems: stability and bifurcation theory, threshold dynamics and numerical simulation.
A central question in my work is how features of real infections change the course of an outbreak. These include memory effects, captured through Caputo fractional derivatives, and incubation and gestation delays. They also include the movement of individuals across space, which can produce Turing patterns, and seasonal variation in transmission. To study seasonality, I am developing non-autonomous models with time-periodic coefficients, in which the basic reproduction number is defined through the next-generation operator of the periodic system. I also work on predator–prey and eco-epidemic systems, where fear, harvesting and disease interact.
Alongside the analysis, I use optimal control, sensitivity analysis and cost-effectiveness analysis to design and compare interventions such as vaccination, treatment and awareness campaigns, with the aim of supporting public-health decisions.

Main themes

  • Fractional-order differential equationsCaputo-type models that carry memory effects into epidemic dynamics.
  • Optimal controlTreatment and vaccination strategies from Pontryagin’s maximum principle, ranked by cost-effectiveness.
  • Spatio-temporal dynamicsReaction–diffusion and cross-diffusion models, Turing instability and pattern formation.
  • Stability, chaos and bifurcationHopf bifurcation, delay-induced oscillations and chaos control in eco-epidemiological systems.
  • Sensitivity analysisPRCC-based global sensitivity analysis and stochastic stability.
  • Data-driven methodsANFIS and genetic-algorithm-based parameter estimation for epidemic trends.
